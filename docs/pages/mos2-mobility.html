0. Summary

On one and the same host and Lanczos chain (2026-08-20 144b22 ns24 sectors, 12×12 coarse mesh × 22 spinor bands, relaxed defects, vacuum-aligned $\Delta V$, 4 symmetry sectors), this page compares four levels of scattering theory — {full $T$-matrix, Born} × {rest self-energy $\Sigma$ kept, dropped} — giving defect-limited mobilities for three defects × electrons/holes (all 12 cells of the matrix delivered), with K-valley zoom spectral functions and the vertex’s Wannier decay providing vector-level evidence for every conclusion. Three headlines: (i) Born is wrong by 1–2 orders of magnitude for deep wells (V$_\mathrm{S}$ holes 53×, strict second order 190×) — the same mechanism as the PdCoO$_2$ vacancy; (ii) $\Sigma$’s role flips with the defect: screening for V$_\mathrm{S}$ (19.9→13.3 eV), building a CBM resonance for O$_\mathrm{S}$ ($\Sigma$(CBM)=42 eV, 4× the bare vertex; dropping $\Sigma$ inflates $\mu_e$ by a spurious 72×), and near-cancellation for Se$_\mathrm{S}$ (3.5 vs 3.12 eV, 0.3–0.6 eV residual); (iii) of the four levels, only full $T$ + $\Sigma$ is simultaneously consistent for all three defects and both carriers. Carrier convention: the sheet density is fixed at $n=10^{10}$ cm$^{-2}$ and $E_F(T)$ is obtained by bisection on the pristine primitive-cell bands; $\mu\propto 1/n_d$.

2a. Why the smallest $\Sigma$ maps do not give the highest mobility: the on-shell audit (2026-08-25)

The $\Sigma$ colour maps of §3 suggest Se$_\mathrm{S}$ is the weakest scatterer on both sides, yet $\mu_h$(Se$_\mathrm{S}$) = 4303 is 3.5× below $\mu_h$(V$_\mathrm{S}$) = 15243. An audit of the mobility energy window (holes: VBM$-$0.36…VBM$+$0.04 eV; on-shell binning $\pm$2.5 meV; out-of-window thermal weight $\sim e^{-13}$) found the window blameless — the resolution is physical, and it is quantified by the on-shell, band-projected linewidths $\hbar\Gamma_\mathrm{qp}=2n_dN_k|\mathrm{Im}\,\tilde\Sigma_{nn}(k,\varepsilon_{kn})|$ in the 300 K transport windows (states along $\Gamma$-K-M; $R_c$=5a cluster = full box; $\eta$=2.5 meV):

Holes: V$_\mathrm{S}$ is nearly transparent at the VBM edge ($\hbar\Gamma$ = 0.04–0.05 meV over the top 40 meV) — its huge vertex lives in the $-0.28$ eV deep resonance and the $+0.02$ eV gap state, both outside the thermal window, and the full-$T$ unitary suppression of the deep well (the same physics that makes Born wrong by 53×) removes the band-edge scattering. Se$_\mathrm{S}$'s only feature — the weak isovalent resonance — sits exactly at the VBM ($\hbar\Gamma$ = 1.25 meV at the edge, 30× V$_\mathrm{S}$), squarely on top of the $e^{-|\varepsilon-\mathrm{edge}|/k_BT}$ weight; thermally averaged, $\langle\Gamma\rangle$ = 0.32 / 0.57 / 1.03 meV for V$_\mathrm{S}$/O$_\mathrm{S}$/Se$_\mathrm{S}$, and the $\tau$-weighting ($\langle 1/\Gamma\rangle \ne 1/\langle\Gamma\rangle$, amplified by V$_\mathrm{S}$'s transparent edge) reproduces the SERTA ratio $\mu_h$(V$_\mathrm{S}$)/$\mu_h$(Se$_\mathrm{S}$) = 6.0. Electrons: O$_\mathrm{S}$'s CBM resonance puts $\hbar\Gamma$ = 3.95 meV right at the edge ($\langle\Gamma\rangle$ = 3.4 meV, 12× the others) — $\mu_e$ last; V$_\mathrm{S}$ and Se$_\mathrm{S}$ are nearly identical (0.25 vs 0.28 meV), matching $\mu_e$ 7654 vs 7412. Why the maps mislead: they show the 22-band trace on a log scale spanning to the 42 eV O$_\mathrm{S}$ peak — transport only sees the band-diagonal projection of the carrier's own band, on shell, within ~3$k_BT$ of the edge. And the on-shell trace? A fair follow-up is that the tables above evaluate all 22 bands at one fixed $\omega$, so 21 of the 22 terms are off shell — what if instead each band is taken at its own quasiparticle energy, $\sum_n\Sigma_{nn}(k,E_{nk})$? Computing that at K (22 separate on-shell evaluations, $R_c$=5a) gives on-shell traces $\sum_n\Gamma_n$ = 20.39 (V$_\mathrm{S}$), 28.84 (O$_\mathrm{S}$), 11.38 meV (Se$_\mathrm{S}$), of which the carrier's own band holds 0.16% / 0.57% / 11.5% at the VBM and 0.79% / 17.6% / 1.4% at the CBM. So the on-shell trace fails to track transport too — it compresses a 40× spread in the VBM channel (Se$_\mathrm{S}$ 1.313 vs V$_\mathrm{S}$ 0.032 meV) into a 1.8× spread and puts V$_\mathrm{S}$ above Se$_\mathrm{S}$ — because it is still dominated by bands the carrier never occupies (V$_\mathrm{S}$: bands 15–16 at $-4.6$ eV, 2.3–2.7 meV, and 31–32 at $+3.4$ eV, 2.9–3.2 meV). Two remarks. First, this quantity is not the trace of any matrix: its terms are evaluated at 22 different arguments, so unlike $\mathrm{Tr}\,\Sigma(k,\omega)$ it has no basis-free meaning — it is well defined only in the band eigenbasis, which $H_k$ singles out. It becomes a genuine invariant only inside a degenerate manifold (the K-point conduction spin pair, the doubly degenerate $\Gamma$ valence maximum), where it is the trace of the decay-rate matrix and equals the sum of the eigenmode widths. Second, the physically natural band sum is over a constant-energy surface, not over bands at fixed $k$: $\sum_{nk}\Gamma_{nk}\delta(E-E_{nk})$ is the total out-scattering rate at energy $E$, tied by the optical theorem to the defect's total cross-section and by its energy integral to the Friedel sum rule — and that is exactly what the $\Gamma(\varepsilon)$ figures below plot. Useful validation: Se$_\mathrm{S}$'s band-26 on-shell width here, 1.313 meV, matches the independent 300×300 grid median at the edge, 1.334 meV. Script diag/onshell_trace.py (Anvil job 20142527).

Kubo versus Boltzmann. A fair objection is that the Kubo formula needs the complete trace, not one band diagonal. It does — but a different trace: $\sigma_{xx}=\frac{\pi\hbar e^2}{\Omega}\int d\omega\,(-\partial_\omega f)\,\mathrm{Tr}[v_x A(\omega)v_x A(\omega)]$ over the full matrix spectral function $A=\frac{i}{2\pi}(G^R-G^A)$, which carries two projections that $\mathrm{Tr}\,\Sigma$ has neither of: the velocity vertices weight each band pair by $|v_{mn}|^2$, and the spectral functions put the weight on shell — a band $\Delta E$ off shell enters with $A_{nn}(\omega)\simeq\Gamma/2\pi\Delta E^2$ instead of $2/\pi\Gamma$. For the bands carrying V$_\mathrm{S}$'s excess trace, $\Delta E$ = 3–7 eV against $\hbar\Gamma\sim0.3$ meV, so the leading interband-coherence correction is $O((\Gamma/\Delta E)^2)\sim10^{-8}$, and the 9–26× trace differences are invisible to any transport formula. Measured (KG bubble on the 300×300 grid, $\eta$=2.5 meV, four levels differing only in what is kept of $\Sigma$ and $v$; Anvil jobs 20149383/84/85). The implementation is validated by a level that keeps both $\Sigma$ and $v$ band-diagonal, which must analytically reproduce SERTA broadened to $\gamma=\Gamma/2+\eta$; it lands within 1.2–7.8% of that reference across the four cases below (the reference is a model Lorentzian built from the on-shell $\Gamma$, so it ignores the Re$\,\Sigma$ shift and the $\omega$-dependence of $\Sigma$ — the largest deviation is O$_\mathrm{S}$ electrons, precisely where the CBM resonance makes $\Sigma(\omega)$ vary fastest and the model is least applicable; that the interband and coherence ratios are identical across all four cases shows this is a limitation of the reference, not of the implementation). Every run gates on hermiticity and non-negative diagonal weight, and all pass (0.0, $+0.8\ldots1.3\times10^{-4}$).

Why $\mu_\mathrm{KG}$ is far below $\mu_\mathrm{SERTA}$, and why that is not a physics statement. Against SERTA with $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma$ (13 183 and 2 830 cm²/Vs) the bubble gives ratios of 0.018 and 0.079 — but that gap is entirely the artificial broadening, not Kubo-versus-Boltzmann: in the bubble the Lorentzian half-width is $\Gamma/2+\eta$, and substituting $\tau=\hbar/(\Gamma+2\eta)$ into the SERTA sum reproduces the bubble to 2% (235.3 vs 239.2; 228.1 vs 223.3). The reason $\eta$ wins is the dilute limit: at $n_d=10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$ the physical $\hbar\Gamma$ is only 0.03–4 meV, so 100% of the 300 K thermal weight, for every defect and both carriers, has $\Gamma<2\eta$. A meaningful bubble would need $\eta\lesssim0.1\,\Gamma\sim5\,\mu$eV with an $\omega$ grid finer still — and in that limit $A$ becomes $\delta$-like and the bubble reduces analytically to SERTA. In the dilute limit the two are therefore not competing numerical schemes; the latter is the analytic solution of the former, which is exactly why the production engine runs an NW ladder at $\eta=600/\mathrm{NW}$ meV and extrapolates linearly in $1/\mathrm{NW}$.

Record of a correction. An earlier revision of this page quoted a coherence correction of 0.12% and a large "interband velocity" enhancement (23×) from this same calculation. Both were wrong: the code built the spectral matrix as the element-wise imaginary part $A=-\mathrm{Im}\,G/\pi$ instead of the anti-Hermitian part $A=\frac{i}{2\pi}(G-G^\dagger)$. Those agree only for complex-symmetric $G$, which $(z-H-\Sigma)^{-1}$ is not when $\Sigma=cN_kT_{kk}$ is non-symmetric; the element-wise form is not Hermitian, admits negative spectral weight, and — decisively — does not transform covariantly under the rotation to the band basis, so taking a band-diagonal of it is meaningless. In a toy case it shifts $\mathrm{Tr}[vAvA]$ by a factor $-10$. It was caught only by adding the band-diagonal-$v$ validation level above, which came out exactly 2× off its analytic target. The fixed code now gates on hermiticity ($\le10^{-12}$) and non-negative diagonal weight at every run; both pass (0.0 and $+1.1\times10^{-4}$).

What the KG bubble cannot deliver here, and why. The absolute ratio $\mu_\mathrm{KG}/\mu_\mathrm{SERTA}$ is not reportable at a numerically accessible $\eta$, and the diagnosis is worth recording. In the KG spectral function the Lorentzian half-width is $\Gamma/2+\eta$, so the artificial broadening competes with the physical linewidth — and at $n_d=10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$ the physical $\hbar\Gamma$ is only 0.03–4 meV, so at $\eta$=2.5 meV 100% of the thermal weight, for every defect and both carriers, satisfies $\Gamma<2\eta$. The consequences are visible in the numbers: the band-diagonal Lorentzian reference becomes nearly defect-independent (220 / 229 / 236 cm²/Vs for the three defects' holes, 328 / 428 / 431 for electrons), $\mu_\mathrm{KG}$ likewise (2592 / 2615 / 2845 and 1429 / 1761 / 1778), and their ratio is a universal 11.7 (holes) / 4.2 (electrons) across three scatterers as different as a 13 eV vacancy well, a CBM-resonant substitutional and an isovalent one. A number that does not distinguish those three is not measuring them — it is measuring $\eta$. A meaningful evaluation would need $\eta\lesssim0.1\,\Gamma\sim5\,\mu$eV and an $\omega$ grid finer still, some 200× the cost; but in that limit $A$ becomes $\delta$-like and the bubble reduces analytically to SERTA plus interband terms suppressed by $(\gamma/\Delta E)^2$. In the dilute limit KG-bubble and SERTA are therefore not two comparable numerical schemes — the latter is the analytic solution of the former, which is precisely why the production engine runs an NW ladder at $\eta=600/\mathrm{NW}$ meV and extrapolates linearly in $1/\mathrm{NW}$ instead of evaluating a bubble. (For completeness: the near-degenerate thermal-weight fraction is 15.9% for holes and 77.7% for electrons — opposite in ordering to the 11.7 / 4.2 ratios, so the interband enhancement in the $\eta$-dominated regime is not simply a degeneracy effect, and no mechanism is assigned to it here.) Scripts diag/kg_bubble.py, data kg_bubble_*.npz / kg_states_*.npz. The one place the two genuinely part company in this material is the near-degenerate conduction spin pair (3 meV splitting versus $\hbar\Gamma$ = 0.3 meV → ~1%, further suppressed because the pair are Kramers partners with small $v_{12}$); Kubo would also be mandatory in regimes this system never enters here — Ioffe–Regel breakdown ($\hbar\Gamma\ll$ the 26 meV thermal window and eV bandwidths, so $k_F\ell\gg1$) or defect-band hopping at much higher $n_d$. Note the converse caveat as well: the bare Kubo–Greenwood bubble carries no vertex correction, so it is the Kubo analogue of SERTA, not of IBTE; matching the IBTE numbers requires the ladder-resummed Kubo, which is diagrammatically the same object as the BTE collision integral.

The grid resolves what a high-symmetry line cannot: the hole side is a $\Gamma$-pocket-versus-K-valley story. The doubly degenerate $\Gamma$ valence maximum sits at $E_\mathrm{VBM}-45.6$ meV, and below it the $\Gamma$ pocket dominates the phase space (the state count per 5 meV bin falls 590 → 72, a factor 6, on crossing it). 2b. The three rates: quasiparticle, momentum relaxation, full IBTE (2026-08-25)

The same 300×300 grid, now carrying the whole ladder the mobility engine solves internally, state by state: the quasiparticle rate $1/\tau_\mathrm{qp}=\Gamma_\mathrm{qp}/\hbar$ (what SERTA uses), the momentum-relaxation rate $1/\tau_\mathrm{tr}=\sum_{s'}P_{ss'}(1-\cos\theta_{ss'})$, and the full linearized-BTE rate $1/\tau_\mathrm{IBTE}=|v|^2/(F\!\cdot\!v)$ with $F=\tau_\mathrm{qp}v+\tau_\mathrm{qp}PF$. The kernel $P_{ss'}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}c\frac{N_k^2}{N_\mathrm{grid}^2}|T_{ss'}|^2L_\eta(\varepsilon_s-\varepsilon_{s'})$ is built from the same $R_c$=5a image-free $T$, with the optical theorem $\sum_{s'}P_{ss'}=\Gamma_s/\hbar$ enforced (median residual 0.2–0.5%).

This is the vertex correction resolved state by state. For Se$_\mathrm{S}$ holes the three separate strongly below the $\Gamma$-pocket edge — window medians 0.740 → 0.300 → 0.247 ps$^{-1}$, a factor 3 — the signature of an isovalent weak perturbation scattering into small angles, where $(1-\cos\theta)$ removes most of the quasiparticle rate. V$_\mathrm{S}$ and O$_\mathrm{S}$ stay nearly degenerate (1.844/1.750/1.710 and 1.394/1.561/1.482 ps$^{-1}$): deep wells scatter near-isotropically, and O$_\mathrm{S}$ holes are even slightly backscattering-dominant ($1/\tau_\mathrm{tr}>1/\tau_\mathrm{qp}$). At the band edges themselves all three converge for every defect, because there only K/K′ intervalley processes survive and those carry $\langle\cos\theta\rangle\approx0$ — no forward-scattering suppression is available. The wide spread of the IBTE points is physical, not numerical: $\tau_\mathrm{IBTE}$ is direction-dependent around the trigonally warped constant-energy contours, whereas $\tau_\mathrm{qp}$ and $\tau_\mathrm{tr}$ are scalars per state.

3a. The O$_\mathrm{S}$ near-VBM "defect flat band": exact-array arbitration (2026-08-24)

At the same $n_d=10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$, the legacy soc-zoom page shows a prominent flat line at $-20$ meV in the O$_\mathrm{S}$ hole window, while the new maps on this page do not. A layer-by-layer audit found: the $A$-assembly formulas of the two generations are identical; no transposition between chain generations (sector vs svdA agree element-wise to 2%, vs 1.8 for the transpose); the support-basis projection residual at the resonance is $3\times10^{-6}$; and re-solving T in the full 3168-dim coarse basis with the legacy-style fine-grid real-space host $g(\Delta R)$ reproduces this page's production numbers exactly ($A=0.0227$ at path idx 100). The two T generations were then put before a zero-approximation referee: at $c=1/144$ (one defect per 12×12 supercell) the chain data itself constitutes a periodic array, and the resolvent of $H_\mathrm{eff}=\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon)+(V_{AA}+\Sigma(\omega))/N_k$ yields the exact $A(k,\omega)$ — no T-matrix equation involved.

Verdict (path idx 100, $\omega-E_\mathrm{VBM}=-20$ meV, $c=1/144$): exact array $A=0.054$; this work's T gives $0.039$ (28% low, tracking the array over the whole $-32\ldots-20$ meV stretch); the published F22 T (RCUT=4 cluster) gives $\mathbf{0.407}$ — a 7.5× spurious resonance peak; the visible flat line in the legacy figure is this truncation artifact. The exact array has no discrete peak at $-20$ meV at this $k$, only a monotonic rise toward the band edge. Same verdict at $k=K$ (right panel, log scale): this work follows the array's band-edge rise while F22 sits flat at 3–4, missing it entirely. Mechanism: the shallow O$_\mathrm{S}$ resonance (binding ~21 meV) has a Bohr diameter of ~12 cells; the 61-cell cluster truncates both the vertex and the host $g$, squeezing the state and inflating its top-valence-band coupling $|\langle K,v|V|d\rangle|^2$ by ~90× (band-projected $-\mathrm{Im}\,\tilde\Sigma$: 17–18 vs 0.2 meV) while leaving the trace nearly unchanged (79.7 vs 72.6 meV) — the trace is invariant under per-$k$ unitary conjugation, which is exactly why "$\Sigma$ traces agree yet $A$ differs" was possible. Diagonalizing $H_\mathrm{eff}$ confirms the $-21.5$ meV localized state is real (a Kramers pair, 67%+31% top-valence-pair composition, participation ratio ≈ 4), but its dilute-limit spectral footprint is intrinsically faint: invisibility in $A$ at $n_d=10^{12}$ is the correct physics (it is visible in the $\Sigma$ maps); for enhanced visibility see the $n_d=10^{13}$ companions and deep-saturation renders below.

3a-bis. Image-free real-space construction ($R_c=3a$, 2026-08-25)

The arbitration above validates the full-box T against the periodic array — but the array itself contains 12×12 periodic-image physics, and the dilute limit calls for an image-free object. Following the user-specified construction, the chain vertex $\tilde V(\omega)=(V_{AA}+\Sigma(\omega))\mathrm{Ry}/N_k$ is Fourier-transformed to the Wannier $(R,R')$ pair representation, truncated to defect-centered cluster sites $|R-R_\mathrm{def}|\le R_c\!=\!3a$ (37 cells; kept matrix elements sit $\ge 9a$ from the nearest image defect), and the T equation is solved with the exact isolated-crystal real-space host $g(\Delta R;\omega)$ from a 480² fine sweep — no BvK torus anywhere downstream of the vertex data. $T(k,k)$ then follows by Bloch sums at arbitrary $k$ (true Wannier interpolation; the window must be centered on the defect cell, auto-detected as $[3,3]$). Verification gates all pass for all three defects: fold identity $\sim10^{-16}$; binned-vs-exact host $g$ $\le1.4\times10^{-3}$; and the untruncated box reproduces the stored k-space full-box $T$ ($\mathrm{Tr}\,T$ rel. dev. $6\times10^{-3}$ / $2\times10^{-3}$ / $3\times10^{-5}$ for O$_\mathrm{S}$/V$_\mathrm{S}$/Se$_\mathrm{S}$).

5. Method and the verification chain

  • Inputs: the 2026-08-20 MODE C chain (144b22 ns24 sectors; NLr 459/459/230/230, herm(A) ~5e-15) plus a w90 rerun on that chain’s own NSCF ($\Omega$=37.519 Ų, agreeing with an independent wannierization to 5 digits) — the u.mat must come from the same NSCF as the chain (gauge continuity).
  • Engine: the $\omega$-resolved vertex is assembled directly in the chain’s Bloch basis (the support basis comes from a Bloch Gram matrix, avoiding an einsum rotation, ~9 s per bin; gT gate: 1.9e-11 element by element against the g8-verified Wannier einsum route); host $g_S$ on a dense 480² spectrum binned at 1.5 meV; a matched-$\eta$ Lorentzian kernel plus optical-theorem enforcement (2 rounds of row scaling, median 0.4–0.8%); and a damped IBTE with the on-shell null mode removed (spectral radius <1, direct and iterative agreeing to ~2e-4).
  • Born variants: a golden-rule kernel $P=|\langle u|\tilde V|u'\rangle|^2$ with $\gamma\equiv\hbar\sum P$ ($s\equiv1$ by construction) — a consistent $O(V^2)$ definition that avoids the mixed orders of $|V+VG_0V|^2$; in the $-\Sigma$ variants the vertex is $\omega$-independent, so the continued fraction is skipped.
  • Gate list: g1 chain eps≡xml (0.0); g2/g2b hr/u.mat ≤0.03 meV; gE (bit-identical, 0.0); gT 1.9e-11; g4 support residual ~1e-6; g8 support vs a full-dimension 3168² direct solve ≤6e-4 (= vertex truncation × $T$ resonance enhancement); g5 optical theorem; g6 IBTE; g7 isotropy; wdecay all LOCALIZED; and Im $T_{nn}\le0$ over the whole mesh.
